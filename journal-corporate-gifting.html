Corporate gifting, without the guesswork

What to send a client versus what to send a team, why the same box rarely works for both, and how to keep a Diwali list of four hundred from becoming a second job.

A client gift and a team gift are different objects

A client gift is doing relationship work. It goes to one named person, it will be seen by their colleagues, and it should contain one item substantial enough to stay on a desk for years — a leather journal, a good pen, a brass object. Cost per unit matters less than the impression a single box makes.

A team gift is doing recognition work at scale. It goes to three hundred people with wildly different tastes, and it needs to land as a genuine gesture rather than a giveaway on every single desk. Here the discipline is the opposite: fewer, better, universally usable items, and a per-unit cost that survives multiplication.

The mistake is buying one hamper and sending it to both. The client thinks it is thin; the finance team thinks it is extravagant.

Running a list of four hundred

Almost all of the pain in corporate gifting is logistics, not selection. Four things reduce it to a single afternoon:

  • One spreadsheet — name, address, city, pincode, phone. Phone numbers matter more than you expect; couriers call before delivery.
  • Decide branding early. Foiling artwork needs to be vector, and approval always takes longer than production.
  • Approve a physical sample before the run. A lid colour on a screen is not a lid colour in a hand.
  • Book the slot. Between Dussehra and Diwali, production capacity across the industry disappears — order six weeks out.

The details that catch people out

Alcohol cannot be shipped between most Indian states and will hold up an entire consignment. Anything requiring refrigeration is out for the same reason. If your recipient list crosses states, keep the contents shelf-stable and you avoid the problem entirely.

On the finance side: get a GST invoice for every order, because gifting spend is claimable and your accounts team will ask for it in April. Confirm whether your organisation has a per-recipient value cap — many regulated sectors do, and it is easier to know before the order than after.

A note on the card

A printed corporate greeting signed by nobody is worse than no card at all. If the managing director will sign three hundred cards, that is the single highest-return thing in the whole exercise. If they will not, print the company name only and leave it at that.

In short: buy two different hampers, order six weeks out, keep everything shelf-stable, and get the invoice.
